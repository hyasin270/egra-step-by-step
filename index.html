Five children on the clock

The target is about five minutes a child, 25–30 for five. Each bar is one child from this run: the bot's own time, the child's speaking time, and the coach's check. The bot is the smallest part.

Bot (prompts, saving, replies)Child speaking (length of the voice notes)Coach's checkFive-minute target

Not in these bars: the coach's spoken cues, handing over the card, and walking a child in and out. The protocol model that adds them puts a child at 6.0 minutes with 60-second quick sums and 5.5 with 30-second quick sums. Waiting for marks (18–29 s a child) is left out because the coach moves on to the next child while it runs.

How close the AI is to the enumerators

Every recording from the May 2026 Rawalpindi study: 185 children, Grades 1–5. Each AI mark is compared with the enumerator's tablet mark for the same child and item. The "human floor" column is how often a QA reviewer, re-listening to the same children, agreed with the enumerator.

The five synthetic children in this run (not an accuracy measure)

Synthetic voices read the card with exact answer keys, and a simulated coach fixes a disagreement 9 times in 10. This shows the check mechanics working. Real children score much lower; use the table above for accuracy.

How this page was made
  • Every chat message is the bot's real outbound WhatsApp payload from the sandbox run of 2 Oct 2026 at 20:32 UTC (01:32 on 3 Oct, Pakistan time), read back from the sandbox logs. Message times are Pakistan time.
  • The simulator sends each voice note as soon as the bot has replied, so the chat clock is compressed. The five-child clock adds each child's real speaking time.
  • The check screens are rendered from roll 8's saved AI marks with the bot's own form code. The card pages are the printed card, form A. The strip photos are rendered test images (crumpled, angled, shadowed, blurred) with a known answer key.
  • Voices are synthetic. No real child's voice, photo or name is on this page.
