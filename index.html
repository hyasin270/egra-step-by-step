What the run found

  • Fixed and confirmed in this run: a finished reader's story speed. The strong child reads the whole Urdu story with about 14 seconds left (65 words a minute). The run before the fix said 177 a minute, because the end of the reading was taken from an earlier copy of the story's last word. In this run the end is where the story's last three words are heard: 15.7 seconds left, 66 and 68 a minute.
  • Open: Urdu listening on a merged recording. The AI scored the strong child 0 of 5 both times: the coach's and the child's voices came out as one speaker, and the grader decided no question was asked. The coach's review restored 5 of 5. The fix finds the questions by their wording, not by who spoke.
  • Open: quick subtractions lined up wrongly. The strong child's recording scored 5 against 15 in both sessions (14 once in the run before): the answers heard were matched to the wrong sums. The fix lines the answers up against the answer key.
  • Open: English letters and made-up words are noisy on these voices (strong child 50 against 58 letters, 22 to 24 against 29 made-up words), and the weak child is over-credited by 3 to 7 on the reading grids. Not yet investigated; real readers are measured on the May recordings.

The 30-minute frontier

How the full battery could fit in 30 minutes

The full battery takes about 24 minutes per child, so about two hours for five children. Two separate analyses of the May data (lanes O1 and O2) looked for every way down to 30 minutes that keeps the items, timing and stop rules intact. They reached the same answer independently.

−2.6 minPer-child shortcuts barely move the clockMeasuring 45 seconds instead of 60 (the rate stays within 1/10 of an SD), dropping one listening question, and skipping the story after a blank first line of words together save 2.6 minutes per child. Five children still take 107 minutes. Removing every non-test second (instructions, tablet time) saves about 6 more.
27 minThe lever is who takes which taskEvery task on every visit, but on different children. Each of the five does one block (Urdu, English, maths number sense, maths operations), and the blocks rotate. Every task is still given in full: 26.8 minutes, about 24 with the shortcuts above.
×1.33What it costs at the level results are usedResults feed ICT and zone trends; the learning-signal study rules out school-level numbers. Children in one class are alike (intra-class correlation .36), so five children from one school are worth about two independent ones. Giving a task to 1–2 children instead of 5 raises the zone-level error by only a third.
56%Why two hours can lose anywayAt about two hours per visit, the full battery only beats the 27-minute design if coaches still make at least 56% of their visits. Fewer visits means fewer schools and weeks, which matter more than more children per school.

Ruled out, with the reason

Written sums while another child is testedEGMA is oral and one-to-one: "no student writing is required" (EGMA Toolkit p.9). Waiting children must not hear the testing (EGRA Toolkit p.99). Written sums measure something else and cannot be pooled with EGMA scores.
Listening to a group at onceEGRA reads the story to one child at a time. In a group, children hear each other's answers. It would save about 6 minutes per language and invalidate the task.
The class teacher as a second assessorA teacher testing their own pupils is not independent, and EGRA requires a trained assessor (90% agreement). A second coach is valid, but costs a second visit.
30-second windows in English, or skipping letters for strong readersEnglish reading slows across the minute, so short windows inflate the rate by 11–18%. Letter speed cannot be predicted from word reading: the school error rises by 0.3–0.5 SD.

Decision for you

Needs a decisionRecommended: every task on every visit, on different children27 minutes (about 24 with the shortcuts), same five-child draw, every task in full. AlternativeOne subject per visitSimplest for the coach: Urdu 30, English 32, maths in two halves of 23 minutes. Each child does a whole subject. But each subject is measured only every few weeks, and the zone error is ×1.8.

FAQ

Your questions, answered

How does EGRA do listening, letter sounds and made-up words? Are they in now?

Yes, all three, for every child. Listening: the coach reads a short story aloud and asks 6 questions (the May study's own, read twice as May did). Letters: 100 letters, 60 seconds, the child names each one; nothing right in the first row stops the task. Made-up words: 50, 60 seconds, the stop rule on the first 5. They test decoding, because a made-up word can't be guessed from its shape. Urdu uses letter names (as Pakistan's Urdu EGRA and the May study do).

What does EGMA actually test? Ours was cut down.

Six skills in eight tasks, 96 items, three of them timed. Number identification (20, 60 s), which is bigger (10), missing number (10), quick additions and quick subtractions (20 each, 60 s each, separately), harder additions and subtractions (5 each, paper allowed), word problems (6, read aloud). v3 gives all eight, with Core EGMA's own items for Grade 3.

Could the next child do written sums on paper while the coach tests another?

No, EGMA does not allow it. It is oral and one-to-one: "no student writing is required. Answers to all items are orally transmitted." Paper is only an optional aid while a child answers aloud. Children waiting must not hear the testing. RTI's written maths tests are separate instruments whose scores are never pooled with EGMA's.

Is maths timed?

Where EGMA times it: number identification, quick additions and quick subtractions, 60 seconds each from the begin line. The rest are untimed and stop after 4 wrong in a row.

Which is bigger: more pairs, and different for Grade 3 and 5?

Ten pairs now. Grade 3 gets Core EGMA's ten. Grade 5 shares the first six (so the grades stay comparable) and gets four harder ones from RTI's official Rwanda Grade 6 items. On the old four pairs, 62% of Grade 5 got everything right; nothing could be learned from them.

Only the questions the child reached?

Done. At "stop" the coach notes where the child's finger is. The message lists each question with the words the child must have read for it, and the score is out of the questions asked.

How long does a visit take now, and how do we get to 30 minutes?

About 24 minutes per child, two hours for five. The path to 30 minutes is above: every task on every visit, on different children, at 27 minutes.

Where do the test items come from? Is anything made up?

Nothing is written new. The May 2026 study where its list survives in full; otherwise RTI's EGRA Toolkit sample grids, the Government of Sindh 2024 Urdu EGRA, Core EGMA, and RTI's Rwanda Grade 6 items. Each item cites its page in the bank, and the grids were checked cell by cell against the source pages.

How does the coach find the children? Roll numbers?

No roll numbers. Full name, the class the way the school writes it, and the class teacher; one button sends each teacher only their own room's children.

Does the coach count words or mark answers?

No. The recording is the score sheet; the AI counts. The coach marks only the few items the recording could not settle, at the end of the visit.

Do the cards change from school to school?

No. EGRA gives every child in a round the same form. One set per term (Set A now); Set B next term, after the pilot equates it.
